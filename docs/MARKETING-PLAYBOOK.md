# Christensen Ranch — Marketing Playbook

*Selling shares of Colorado-raised beef, delivered as a cut sheet the customer designs themselves.*

---

## 1. Positioning

**One sentence:** Steakhouse cuts at a rancher's price — beef that never crosses a state line, one decision, one honest per-pound number.

**The three claims everything hangs on:**

1. **The discount is the headline.** One all-in price (~$9/lb on a half) for everything in the box, when the store charges $18–25/lb for the ribeyes and filets that make up a third of it. Lead every ad, page, and pitch with this comparison — "ribeye at a rancher's price."
2. **One decision, zero homework.** No cut sheets, no per-cut menu. Pick a size, pay a deposit, get the butcher's proven standard cut. Simplicity is the counter-position to both grocery stores (price) and other ranches (complexity).
3. **Colorado farm-to-table, literally.** One animal, one family, one short drive. Feedlot beef changes hands ~6 times; ours changes hands once — and you pay only for the pounds your animal actually yields.

**Price framing rule:** never lead with the $1,000+ ticket. Lead with *$/lb in your freezer* against the store's per-cut prices, and *feeds a family of four for a year*. The deposit ($200–$600) is the only number that has to feel small.

## 2. Audiences

| Segment | Who | Hook | Where they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freezer families | Front Range parents, 30–55, own a chest freezer or will buy one | "A year of dinners, solved" + per-lb math | Facebook groups, Nextdoor, school networks |
| BBQ hobbyists | Smoker owners, cut-obsessed | Packer brisket, custom thickness ribeyes, patty program | BBQ Facebook/Reddit, r/Denver, butcher counters |
| Values buyers | Know-your-farmer, anti-feedlot, homesteaders | The "never crosses a state line" story | Farmers markets, homesteading groups, local food co-ops |
| **Group organizers** | The one friend who organizes everything | Split-a-cow: "get the whole-beef price without the whole cow" | They find *you* — give them tools |

Group organizers are the growth engine, not a segment. One organizer = 3–4 customers, pre-trusted, at near-zero CAC.

## 3. The Split-a-Cow referral loop (core growth engine)

The mechanics are already built into the site:

1. Organizer starts a group → gets a share link.
2. Friends claim quarters, each pays separately (nobody fronts money — this removes the #1 group-buy objection).
3. When the cow completes, **everyone's rate drops to the whole-beef price** (~$120 back per quarter). The discount only exists if the group fills — every member is now recruiting.
4. The group page shows the cow literally filling up. Scarcity + progress + social proof in one image.

**Amplifiers to add over time:**
- **Organizer perk:** free organs/bones upgrade or $50 off for whoever starts a completed group. Cheap, and flags your best evangelists.
- **Completion push:** automated email/SMS when a group hits 3/4 — "one quarter left on your cow, deadline Oct 8." Highest-converting message you will ever send.
- **Post-pickup ask:** 2 weeks after pickup, email "How was the brisket?" → review request + "start next season's group" CTA.
- **K-factor target:** ≥1 new group spawned per 2 completed groups. Track it; it's the whole business.

## 4. Channels (in priority order)

1. **Google Business Profile + local SEO** — "beef shares Colorado", "quarter cow Denver", "half beef Greeley". Buyers search exactly these phrases with wallet in hand. The FAQ section on the site is written to win these queries; add a city-targeted FAQ/blog page per major town (Greeley, Fort Collins, Denver, Longmont).
2. **Nextdoor + local Facebook groups** — the natural habitat of "anyone know a good beef guy?" threads. Don't advertise; answer. One helpful comment with the split-a-cow link outperforms paid.
3. **Butcher partnership** — Colorado Custom's counter is a lead source: cards/QR at pickup ("liked your beef? reserve next season's animal"), and they refer walk-ins who want cut-to-order.
4. **Farmers markets (seasonal)** — not to sell meat; to collect deposits. Bring the cut diagram on a banner, run the cut-sheet builder on an iPad, take $200 deposits on the spot.
5. **Email + SMS lifecycle** — deadline-driven. Four harvests a year = four natural campaigns: "Order by Sep 8 for September beef. 3 shares left." Real scarcity, real dates, no manufactured urgency.
6. **Local media/newsletters** — Denver/NoCo food newsletters and TV love a "family ranch does direct-to-consumer with a build-your-own cut sheet" story. Pitch once per season.
7. **Paid (later)** — Meta lead ads geo-fenced to Front Range, creative = the cow diagram + "$0 until you choose every cut" framing. Only after organic + referral loops are humming.

## 5. Content that does the selling

- **The price-math content** — side-by-side receipts: "this week's meat counter run vs. the same cuts from our box." The comparison table on the How It Works page, screenshotted everywhere.
- **What's-in-the-box explainers** — the clickable steer map ("tap the rib: 8–12 ribeyes in a half") as short videos/reels.
- **Freezer math calculator** — already on the site (per-lb all-in, per share size).
- **Harvest diary** — one photo email per season stage (pasture → trailer → dry-age room → boxes). Farm-to-table proof, zero writing effort.
- **Recipes by cut** — one per decision section; doubles as SEO surface ("what to do with beef shank").

## 6. Seasonal calendar (built-in urgency)

| When | Beat |
|---|---|
| 5 wks before deadline | "Reservations open for [Month]" email + socials |
| 2 wks before | Split-a-cow push — "grab neighbors, save ~$120 each" |
| 1 wk before | Scarcity: real share count remaining |
| Deadline week | Last call (email + SMS) |
| Drop day | Harvest diary content |
| Pickup week | Logistics email (coolers, freezer space) — reduces no-shows |
| +2 weeks | Review ask + next-season group CTA |

**November/December is the Super Bowl** — holiday prime rib + gifting + New-Year stock-up. Hold back inventory for it.

## 7. KPIs

- Deposits per harvest (the only number that matters)
- % of orders that came through a group link (target: 40%+ by year one)
- Groups started → completed rate (target: 60%+; chase every 3/4 group personally)
- Email list growth per market/event
- All-in CAC by channel (should be near-zero for referral/organic; cap paid at ~$60/order)
- Repeat rate season-over-season (bulk beef lives or dies on retention; 50%+ is achievable)

## 8. 30 / 60 / 90

**Days 1–30 — Foundation.** Confirm real pricing (kill the placeholders), refund policy, storage terms. Stand up Google Business Profile, claim socials, launch the site on a real domain with Stripe deposits live. Seed 3 pilot groups from friends/family to generate real photos and testimonials.

**Days 31–60 — Local proof.** First farmers-market booth with iPad ordering. Butcher-counter cards live at Colorado Custom. Publish 4 cut-explainer posts + city SEO pages. Start the lifecycle emails for the next harvest deadline.

**Days 61–90 — The loop.** Turn on 3/4-group nudge automations and the organizer perk. Pitch two local newsletters. Review-ask automation. Measure group-attributed revenue; double down on whatever fills cows.
