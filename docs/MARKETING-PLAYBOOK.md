# Thunderbolt Ranch — Marketing Playbook

*Gen 1: one October 2026 harvest, custom-cut Angus beef, ranch to table. Order by Sept 30.*

---

## 1. Positioning

**One sentence:** Get to know your beef — one Colorado Angus animal, cut your way, at $6/lb hanging weight.

**The three claims everything hangs on (all substantiated by the one-pager):**

1. **One animal, not a blend.** Store beef is a blend of dozens of animals; your steaks, roasts, and ground all come from one Angus, pasture-raised in Colorado and grain-finished for marbling. Ownership from conception to harvest — no sale barns, no middlemen.
2. **Beats the grocery store on price *and* provenance.** ≈$8.57/lb take-home vs. the USDA national average of $10.47/lb for Choice — for typically Choice-or-Prime beef, in a full mix of steaks, roasts, and ground instead of whatever's on sale.
3. **Your beef, your way.** A guided cut sheet — steak thickness, roast sizes, ground ratio — with every cut explained in plain English, filled onto the butcher's official form automatically. Nobody else makes custom cutting this easy.

**Language rules:** Grain Finished · Pasture Raised · Ranch to Table · Colorado cattle, Colorado ranch, Colorado butcher. Never claim anything beyond the one-pager (no feedlot talk, no "born on our ranch," no unverifiable sourcing claims).

**Price framing:** lead with $6/lb hanging and the $8.57 vs. $10.47 USDA comparison. The $250 deposit — same for every size — is the number that makes starting feel easy. Balance due at pickup, checks to Thunderbolt Ranch LLC.

## 4. Channels (deadline-driven, in priority order)

1. **Word of mouth + Josh's network** — Gen 1 is a single harvest; a handful of texts and calls can sell it out. The site is the closer: send the link, the wizard does the explaining.
2. **Google Business Profile + local SEO** — "quarter cow Colorado," "half beef Greeley," "custom cut beef Kersey." The How It Works page is written to answer these searches.
3. **Nextdoor + local Facebook groups** — answer "anyone know a good beef source?" threads with the link; don't advertise.
4. **Colorado Custom's counter** — cards/QR at the butcher for walk-ins who want a whole-animal share next time.
5. **Email/text the list** — three beats: reservations open → two weeks out ("cut sheets due Sept 30") → final week countdown. Real dates, real scarcity, no manufactured urgency.
6. **Local food media** — "Colorado ranch sells custom-cut Angus direct, cheaper than the grocery store average" is a pitchable story after Gen 1 proves out.

## 5. Content that does the selling

- **The steer map** — tap-a-primal yield popups; screenshot/screen-record it for social.
- **The wizard walkthrough** — a 60-second screen recording of "9–15 ribeyes at 1-inch… 4–8 at 2-inch" is the whole pitch for BBQ people.
- **The price math** — $8.57 take-home vs. $10.47 USDA Choice average, side by side.
- **From Pasture to Freezer** — the three-weights graphic (1,500 → 900 → 630 lb) educates away the #1 confusion (hanging vs. take-home) before it becomes a phone call.
- **Harvest updates** — a photo email at kill, mid-hang, and packaging keeps buyers engaged through the two-week wait.
